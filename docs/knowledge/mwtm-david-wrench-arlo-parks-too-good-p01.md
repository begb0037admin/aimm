## Chunk 2

Hello, I'm David Wrench, welcome to Mix with the Masters. Today we'll be looking at my mix of the Arlo Parks track, Too Good, and we'll be breaking it down to its elements, looking about getting various elements of space into the track and the vocal to sit in there, and then maybe looking at what's going on in the master bus. ## Chunk 4

## Chunk 5

## Chunk 6

And it takes a while. I always find it takes about a year from being in a room before you can really settle into where the right spots are for things, especially for recording. Maybe you can get the control room sounding good quite quickly, but it takes quite a bit of experimentation to find where the best place for drums is, where the guitar amps sound good, where is the best place to record vocals. But I think we're there now. I've done sessions here with a full live band, and I've produced a few albums here, and it's a nice environment to work in, and it's definitely quiet from outside noise, which is a big bonus.

## Chunk 7

I'm still using the same speakers that I've been using for years. So it's a pair of Neumann KH-310As and a big Neumann sub that I'm almost sat on. It's right under the desk here, right by where my knees are, because I really like to feel the sub when I'm mixing. And then my alternative speakers are these Unity Audio Rock 2s, and I'll occasionally use some Audis-y headphones to check mixes as well. And then my little, fewer radio that's down here. So that's, yeah, that's my, but mainly it's the Neumanns. That's, they're my main setup.

## Chunk 8

I'm about to have an Atmos system put in, but that'll be built around the Unities and some Neumanns up in, smaller Neumanns up in the ceiling. But I've used the Neumanns for years. I just find they're really accurate, especially for more electronic music. And paired with the sub, I know I can trust what's happening in the low end, and I'm really confident about it. Actually, this is the best room I've had them sound in, because when you sit here, the stereo image is really spot on. It's really, there's an amazing separation and distance. I found that you have to angle them in slightly for them to sound right. They don't sound great when they're straight on. Angling them in really opens up the stereo image and makes it really correct and much easier to space things out and get the mixes right.

## Chunk 9

I generally mix in the box. It's rare that I'll go out into a desk, and I wouldn't do that in this room anyway. I'd go to a different room. But these days, with what's needed for recalls and stems, working in the box is the only way that makes sense time-wise for me. Plus, once I did some tests, I couldn't really hear much different. I wasn't gaining much from going through. I wasn't gaining anything, actually, from going through a desk. It was, in fact, in blind tests, I preferred stuff that was mixed in the box. It had slightly more clarity to it.
