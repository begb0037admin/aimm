# Using the Dynamics on a SSL Console — Part 1: Using The Dynamics On A SSL Console

Source: MWTM set record: docs/mwtm/sets/Andy_Wallace_Using_The_Dynamics_On_A_SSL_Console_Interview.md (local recording, no public URL)
Channel: Mix With The Masters

## Chunk 1

I suspect I don't use compression quite as much as a lot of other people do. I'm more apt to use it for establishing a sound than for leveling. I try to do most of my leveling with actual fader rides and such. As far as the sound, I'll typically use a fairly fast attack, for instance, on a snare or something if I want to get that tighter sound. I don't know how to describe it sonically. I do find that I often use a fairly high ratio approaching limiting. Again, I often paint in kind of broad strokes. So if I'm experimenting with something to see what sound I will get out of it, I'll often go to a higher ratio and be kind of extreme and see if it's moving in a direction I like and then maybe back off on that, back off on either the amount of compression or the ratio.

## Chunk 2

I will also use compression on vocals to some degree. And they're usually a little more delicate. I'm less apt to be hitting a limiter really hard with a vocal. I'm using it more. I mean, I suppose in that instance I am using that, at least more so than with drums, using it as a leveling device. But again, I try to do most of that with writing, even on lead vocals. If there's a note that pops out, I'll do that. I'll write it.

## Chunk 3

I find I'm not doing a lot of compression on bass anymore. I'm more apt to be... One of the issues that I at least find happens a lot with bass is certain notes will stick out more than others or get lost. Sometimes it's in the nature of that particular instrument. Sometimes it has to do with what else is around it in the mix. You know, guitars or keyboards or vocals or whatever. But if I need to get certain notes to be more present or less present, again, I am more apt to do that with writing than with compressing because writing, you're just changing the level and it's louder or not. Compression, if you're using compression for leveling purposes, it tends to change the sonic aspect of it too. And I find sometimes it changes it in a way that I don't want it to change, so better to do it with writing.

## Chunk 4

Continuing with some explanation about the compression that I'm using on the drums, I do, again, early on when I'm setting up and listening to sounds, I'll experiment sometimes with fairly radical compression just to kind of hear what it's doing to it and see if it's something I can use or something I'm interested in. There are times when, rather than experimenting and seeing what's there, I'll actually be looking for something from a compressor saying, well, there's something about this that I want to change or I want to look for, you know, a harder, a punchier sound on it where I'll then actually specifically look for what I'm looking for by using it as opposed to just experimenting and saying, well, what's there? You know, what have we got to play with?

## Chunk 5

Again, in the process of painting with rather broad strokes, I am apt to end up using a fairly high ratio on certain things. On this particular mix here, I have on the kick-out mic, I have a... It's not full-out limiting, but it's pretty high ratio. I rarely look at the numbers. Same thing with the EQs. I rarely look at, you know, well, what frequency am I dealing with? You know, I mean, obviously I know what stage of the EQ I'm working with, but it's more a matter of listening.

## Chunk 6

The idea of using a higher or a lower ratio and making up for it with the gain. As best as I can say for how I use it, the higher ratio gives it more of a pop, I guess I'd call it. The lower ratio is a gentler approach toward that. So, and again, I'm more apt to use a compressor for the sound as opposed to leveling. So, I guess I do occasionally end up with a high ratio because it's more audible. It's more of an audible effect. More often than not, that's what I'm looking for, an audible effect as opposed to a leveling effect on it. With the higher ratio, you're apt to get that.

## Chunk 7

One of the things, though, that you do have to be careful is, or I have to be careful, is when you are using the compressor, the SSL specifically, with a high ratio, sometimes you get more pop and punch, but it can also make the sound somehow smaller. So, you have to find that balance, otherwise you end up with something that's got real pop to it, but it's not a big sound anymore. If you don't want to wipe it out, you have to be careful about that.

## Chunk 8

As far as the metering on the compressors are concerned, I don't pay too much attention. If I see the red lights on the, you know, the red LEDs are blinking a lot, chances are it's too extreme and altering the sound too much unless you're really going for something radical. But I don't have, again, I don't have a rule of thumb about, oh, I want to see so many lights blinking. I would say, in general, I try to avoid the red lights blinking. It's a little bit like the ratio, I guess. If I'm really looking to alter the sonic aspect of the sound, you'd have to look, I guess, to determine that. But I would guess, in general, if I'm trying to do that, that I probably have, you know, three of the, what is it, there's three green lights, so I might have two or three of those going. If I'm trying to be gentle about it, I'll probably be using less of the compression.
