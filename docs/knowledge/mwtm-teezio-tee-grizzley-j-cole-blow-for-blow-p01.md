## Chunk 8

Hi-hats, because there's so much high in them, they tend to stick out a lot, which is where then I come in with a soothe. And I usually, with the percentage, I'm usually lowering it definitely below 100 unless it's like really, really crazy. So I'm at like 82% sharpness at 5.3. And I'm essentially just getting, again, that area that's sort of sticking out and peeking like a pyramid. I'm just trying to just soften the hi-hat a little bit because I don't want the hi-hat to interfere with the vocal. So when I'm mixing, everything is about the vocal. It's about getting these elements to cut through but also not stand in the way of the vocals. Something like the hi-hat or the clap is in the frequency range of messing with the vocal. So with this, I'm trying to still have it be bright, but I'm trying to have it also be toned back a little bit.

## Chunk 9

After the clap, we're going to get into the 808, which I'm assuming is everyone's favorite part is the 808 and how hard it hits. So let's solo that out. So it's a lot harder than the original without anything on it. EQ-wise, I know that with this record, this 808 had so many, so many low frequencies in it that it was actually just too much. So I actually cut it off and I put the EQ in natural phase. So that's going to allow the low end when I do this cut not to sound weird and phasey. And I actually had to make that decision when I was mixing it because when I'm choosing whether I want to put something in natural phase or just leave it in how it is static, I'm usually listening to see if there's any difference, right? So when I first pulled it up, I cut it and I probably heard some issues going on with the phase. So I switched it to natural phase and that actually helped the problem. But you can see I'm cutting essentially everything below 25 on this 808. But you can hear how necessary it is with how much low end this record has.

## Chunk 10

After that, we're hitting it with the Pro-Q2, I think... Or no, Pro-Q3. So after I did this, I left that and I did a couple more notches down. I'm doing 1.2 down at 67 and 142 hertz down 0.83 dBs. ## Chunk 11

After that, we're running it into the Neutron 3. Let's see how much compression we're getting here. I'm probably doing about 3.2 dBs of reduction. I have the attack lowered. This comes at 20 on the attack and I lowered it down to 4. So it's a pretty fast attack. But this 808 also does have a pretty hard transient on it as well. So I'm doing about 3 dBs of reduction on it to just get it to sit in the pocket because it was definitely a lot crazier of an 808 before. And then even after that, I'm still hitting it with more compression just to set it in more. About another dB and a half there of compression. Don't be afraid to compress your 808s as long as it's still hitting and it's nice. I mean, a lot of times with 808s and with vocals, it's a battle because the 808 is going to occupy so much room within the mix that the vocal won't have anywhere to sit. So again, what I was saying before is it's all about their lyrical content and the artist. The 808 just needs to be hitting hard enough to not be in the way of the vocal essentially.
