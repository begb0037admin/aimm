## Chunk 1

Now it's time to get to percussion and as you'll see there's quite a bit of percussion. This is one of the things about Kaleo is they build what sound like very, very simple songs. If you listen to this song, which you just did, you'll hear drums, you hear some percussion, sort of typical stomp clap stuff, you'll hear guitars and you hear some voices. And there are definitely some background vocals, a few keyboards, but it doesn't sound like there is a huge amount going on, and there is. This band is the king of subtle little quiet things that when you take them out, you miss them, but you don't necessarily notice. So there's a lot of stuff. Some of it is things going together to make one thing and some of it is just little stand alone things that within the context of the song help the groove along.

## Chunk 6

So all of this goes together to come out of the first chorus, get to the second verse, and now we have some new elements. This in terms of songwriting and arranging is, I would say in a lot of ways, the most difficult part of any song is coming from the first chorus, where you've finally gotten the payoff for whatever the first verse is, back down to the second verse. You need to get the listener to start listening to the story again instead of being swept up in the chorus, which may have a repeating lyric, like this song. And now, all of a sudden, you're going to get wordy again. You've got to listen, but it's also musically much smaller. So introducing something, or lots of things, or changing the sound of something, is a way to get people to pay attention again. It's almost like they zoom out, hear the whole song, and now, boom, I'm back and I'm listening to the vocal. But it isn't exactly the same as the section before. So some very clever arrangement just in the percussion to do that.
