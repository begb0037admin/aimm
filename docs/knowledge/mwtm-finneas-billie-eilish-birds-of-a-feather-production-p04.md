# Birds Of A Feather — Part 4: Verse 2 Arrangement Strategies

## Chunk 1

Moving into verse two, this is kind of a general rule of thumb for verse twos. You either have to reinvent the wheel or you got to take a bunch of stuff out, but that's where you live. So in this case you can see from the sort of existence of the tracks that mostly it's just taking stuff out and letting the vocal and the drums rock again. There's a couple of things doing different stuff than they were doing. So that bass guitar that in the first half of the song is 16th notes picked on the second verse just doing big drops, just dropping off the neck. On each of the chord changes, which has a kind of a nice feel.

## Chunk 2

The synth that we've been hearing the entire song, this kind of sad, melancholy, that synth is totally dropped out. The only synth that you're hearing is the washy synth. And Billie again is like using all this empty space that we've created to do these cool little vocal moments, which is the whole reason for giving yourself space there anyway. And interacting with the delay, which she's hearing in her headphones or in the room. And letting that echo bounce off of itself, which I love.

## Chunk 3

Halfway through this verse. Another sort of element added is really just her voice processed. She says full of shit and you hear this epic delay trem. We've got a really strong overdrive into a room, into a tremolator, into a delay. All happening on you're so full of shit. Just to emphasize, just to exist there and do its job.

## Chunk 4

It's a great little drum fill from Andrew here. Super easy. Just two hits on the snare, hit on the rack tom. Love it. Some piano stuff. A chopped up piano part, admittedly, but a piano part nonetheless. And then that crazy synth, that is the sample chop. And that kind of like volume swell is just changing the velocities on the arp. So you're seeing that the arp is just registering with kind of just ramps. Because it sounded cool. Piano. Not something that you're really hearing, but you're feeling it.

## Chunk 5

Now, structure wise. Again, the thing that we're calling the pre-chorus. We skip. We go straight to what we're calling the chorus. And we fully drop out the drums here. To just really emphasize the lyrical content. To emphasize the change of delivery. You're going to deliver a lyric differently over no drums than you are drums. And to let that under lick shine. We have the tambourine, but no kick or snare.

## Chunk 7

I bounce in and out of the room while Billie records vocals these days. I've spent the last couple of years teaching her how to vocal engineer herself because she's very particular and loves to do a million takes and loves to comp them herself, and everybody wins if she's doing the work. The other element is, even though we're very comfortable around each other, to hit this insane delivery right here, you've got to really go for it. And sometimes, the same way that most people sing in the shower or in their car alone, doing something and not having to feel self-conscious about it allows you to fail harder.

## Chunk 8

Kind of a testament to sort of the benefits of having her know what she's doing on her own, tracking it herself.
