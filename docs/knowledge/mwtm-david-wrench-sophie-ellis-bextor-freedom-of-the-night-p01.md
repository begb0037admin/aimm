# Freedom Of The Night — Part 1: Introduction Production Constraints Percussion Songwriting Mixing EQing Editing

Source: MWTM set record: docs/mwtm/sets/David_Wrench_Sophie_Ellis_Bextor_Freedom_Of_The_Night_Mixing.md (local recording, no public URL)
Channel: Mix With The Masters

## Chunk 1

Hi, I'm David Wrench and I'm back here with Mixed With The Masters in The Boy I'd Paris. Today I'm going to be opening up a session that I mixed and did some additional production on for Sophie Alice Baxter called Freedom of the Night. It came out in the summer of 2024. I believe it's healing me. All I need is sleep release and the freedom of the night, freedom of the night.

## Chunk 2

So, Sophie, we haven't worked together before this. I've been aware of Sophie for many, many years because she had huge hits back in the 90s and one of those hits, Murder on the Dance Floor, became a huge viral hit for a second time last year on the back of the film Salt Plum. Where it was used to an end sequence that then went really viral on all of the social media. Sophie's made consistently brilliant records throughout her whole career and has worked in all sorts of different styles.

## Chunk 3

And coincidentally, with the timing of this viral hit, which was one of her earlier tracks, she was already working on some more disco-influenced, pop-influenced tracks ready for her next album, having done music that was maybe a bit more stripped down and acoustic for a couple of albums. So, the timing was perfect and I went and met Sophie last summer and her partner Richard. We just talked about music for a bit, really. We talked about a love of disco records and what really makes you want to dance when you listen to a record.

## Chunk 4

I think the thing we really agreed on and bonded over was the fact that for music to really make you move and to really make you want to dance, it can't be too rigid. Something has to be a little bit off on it. On all the best dance records, there's a human element to it. There's a shuffle. It's not entirely perfect. There's a roughness to it. And some of this had carried over, I think, into the music she'd been working on.

## Chunk 5

So, for this track, I think it was written very quickly. It just came out of a writing session. And I think they'd been working with parts that came from the original writing session, even though the production had been done on it. And then there was a sudden rush because it had to be used for an advert campaign for a clothing brand. So, we had to finish the track quickly to tie in with that. And it probably came to me slightly earlier than it would have had that not been pressing.

## Chunk 6

So, because of that, it wasn't 100% finished. So, I knew I was going to have to do some additional production to try and tie the whole track in. Even though everything that was on there was really good, it needed a little bit of roughing up and it wasn't quite finished in terms of structure.

## Chunk 7

When it came to me, it had the structure of an intro, first verse, chorus, second verse, chorus, and then it finished. My first thoughts were, okay, well, this just doesn't feel like a finished structure. It was very short. It didn't quite feel satisfying. And it didn't feel like it lifted towards the end. It was obvious the chorus needed to come back. And it was obvious that something needed to happen between the second chorus and it coming back as a third chorus.

## Chunk 8

It also felt like it was missing some percussion drive because a lot of the percussion and the drums were done from the original writing session. It felt a little bit stiff and like it needed roughing up a bit on top of that. So, I aim to do that. And it also felt like harmonically it just could have been a bit bigger and a bit stronger and that there was probably going to need some more additional vocals as well. So, it became quite a bit of back and forth.

## Chunk 9

So, when we look at the files I've got here, what happened with the process is I initially sketched out a new structure. I didn't really get a ref mix with this. I had heard I'd been played one, but because they didn't want to stick to the ref at all, I wasn't really sent it. I was just sent files. Now, I haven't got those files with me because the session morphed from one thing to another.

## Chunk 10

So, just to explain what I'm actually working with here today, I was sent original files that I then sort of EQ'd and I edited quite heavily. And then at one point, I then sent all those files back to Sophie and she went and did additional work on them and then sent them back to me and I worked from them again. So, there was quite a bit of back and forth. So, it's not like a usual standard mix really.

## Chunk 11

However, I took a lot of the processing off the files I sent her to work with so that I could maintain my processing here because as you'll see, there's a fair bit of side-chaining happening and I didn't want to have to print that on and then go back to that. So, I worked on edits essentially, sent the raw files back edited and then they came back to me again with some more structural changes that they did.

## Chunk 12

I'll just play you where it used to end. So, coming out of the second chorus. And so, it would finish there at that point. So, I was like, well, what happens there? I just felt it needed to drop down and then it could continue with the chords. So, I looped some chords around from the chorus. I'll show you what I originally did because I do still have that in. So, let's mute the vocal that was then added in because this vocal wasn't there at that point. So, there wasn't a guitar solo there either at that point.

## Chunk 13

What I did to sketch out my idea was I just played like a one-take rough Moog solo at this point which was just to give an idea of what I was thinking should happen there. It was like, this is a sketch of what could happen here not that I'm saying this is it. So, I wanted to build in energy at this point because she's singing about energy she's singing about something being very euphoric so, I knew it needed something to lift and build there and I sent that as a draft idea of vaguely what needed to happen there.
