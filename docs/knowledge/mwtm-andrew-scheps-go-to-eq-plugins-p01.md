# Go-to EQ Plugins — Part 1: Go To EQ Plugins

Source: MWTM set record: docs/mwtm/sets/Andrew_Scheps_Go_To_EQ_Plugins_Interview.md (local recording, no public URL)
Channel: Mix With The Masters

## Chunk 1

What are your favorite EQs? My favorite EQs. There's no favorite, but there are lots of different EQs I use, some of them just different types and some of them very specific. So if I am going to EQ something to fix a problem, I almost always use the Avid EQ3. You can solo the bands if you hold shift and control and move any knob. It turns it into a notch filter so you can find the problem frequencies and things like that. And it's a very flexible, good EQ that just sounds like nothing. It just does what it says it's supposed to do.

## Chunk 2

Then the rest of the time I'm usually EQing for character. So I love the 1073 style EQs are one of my favorites because that's what I grew up on. It was old Neve and I spent a lot of time on the Shep's plug-in, so I'll plug my plug-in to Shep 73. I use that a lot for many reasons, but mostly it's just those frequencies sound good to me when I'm EQing. And that's almost always for boosting and then using the high-pass filters. So I don't take away on that type of EQ.

## Chunk 3

Then also the Pultec style EQs if you just want something different. I also like the API 560 style EQ to have a graphic EQ that has a variable bandwidth. So the more you boost or cut, the tighter the bandwidth becomes. So you can either do some very broad, almost Pultec style EQing by using three or four adjacent bands and not going too far with them. Or you can really nail a frequency in the guitar and crank it up where you're not affecting the other frequencies around it. So you can kind of poke a hole in a mix with that.

## Chunk 4

Then for the mix I use something that's even more surgical like the BX-V2. And I think that's it. And I try to not EQ things by default. I only EQ because I really feel like I need to change the tone of something. I won't just automatically put an EQ on something. I'll try to do it with level or panning before EQing.

## Chunk 5

One of the things that I use a lot are high-pass filters. And one of the characteristics about high-pass filters is the steeper the slope, the more of a bump you actually get before it starts to go down. So if you look at an EQ curve for a low-pass filter and change from 3 dB per octave up to 24 dB per octave, what you'll start to see is before it goes down, it goes up. So you actually get a bump at a frequency and then it starts to take away low end. So you can use that in conjunction with the low-mid boost like a Neve-style EQ to get a really solid low end by filtering just below the frequency you want. So let's say you have your high-pass filter set at 60 Hz. You're actually getting a really big bump if it's a steep filter at around 70 Hz before it goes back down.

## Chunk 6

So to be specific about which plugins I would use for the different types of EQ, for the Pultec style I would go either for the Waves PuigTech or the UAD Pultec. I think that the Waves straight Pultec emulation is very good, but the PuigTech has a little more character. As with most of the Puig plugins, they've gone for sort of a unique version of that box. They're not trying to be a general version. It's a very specific one. And the UAD plugin sounds really good, so I use that as well.

## Chunk 7

For the Neve-style plugin, obviously I prefer the Shep 73 over all others because it is the best. And one of the main reasons is it's sort of the opposite of what I was just saying about the PuigTech. We were trying very hard to make it sound like a great 1073 but not like a specific 1073 because they can have very individual characteristics to them. And we just wanted this one to sound like you had a console full of them. So if you use the plugin 10 times in a session, you wouldn't start to hear the thing that was a little bit weird about it, which I hear when I use some of the other emulation plugins. Stuff starts to build up at very specific frequencies because that's the frequency that had the most harmonic distortion. So we tried to balance that out.

## Chunk 8

But that said, a lot of the other Neve emulations are good too. Because for me, it's much more about the style of EQ than the actual EQ plugin. So don't get hung up too much on like, oh, my mixes would be better if I had this specific plugin. But just buy the Shep 73. Other than that, you can use anything you want.

## Chunk 9

For the more surgical stuff, I really love the Avid EQ3. It's amazing. It does exactly what it needs to do. And again, the BXV2 is good if you really, really, really want to tweak because you've got 5-band with two filters, MS, kind of anything you could want to do inside of one plugin for EQ processing.

## Chunk 10

And then for graphic EQs, I love the 560. The Waves and the UAD sound very different from each other. I think I probably use the Waves a little bit more. It feels a little bit grainier to me, which I'm usually using it for the mid-range bands. And then so I like to have that grain because that's what I'm trying to get. It's usually on a guitar or a piano, something like that. I really want it to stick out. So I'll usually use that one.

## Chunk 11

The difference between the Waves Puig Tech and the UAD Puig Tech to me, I guess, is that maybe the UAD Puig Tech sounds more like I'm used to having the actual boxes sound. And the Puig Tech is much more of a specific Pultec. But what I don't necessarily like about the UAD one, which is more true to the hardware, is it can be very messy. The low end can, it's very, very broad. And it can almost ring. Like when you're EQing at 60, there's stuff going on at 30, which is what the actual hardware does.

## Chunk 12

And in the hardware, I actually prefer the Lang Pultec style EQ, which is a solid state version of the EQ. The EQ is passive in both of those boxes. But the amplifiers are tube and the Puig Tech in solid state and the Lang. And I feel like the Lang is actually tighter. Whereas the Puig Tech, you add a bunch of low end to a kick and it just kind of goes crazy. The Waves Puig Tech plug-in has a little bit less of that, just because Jack's Puig Tech had less of that for some reason. And the Lachie Zистin.
