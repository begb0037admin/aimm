# Birds Of A Feather — Part 5: Novel Songwriting Approaches Third Album

Source: MWTM set record: docs/mwtm/sets/Finneas_Billie_Eilish_Birds_Of_A_Feather_Production.md (local recording, no public URL)
Channel: Mix With The Masters

## Chunk 1

I think in terms of, you know, where you go on a third album, the first thing that was important to us was just that we were making stuff that inspired us, that felt like we were creating new material. You know, your first album you're using chord progressions for the first time, you're using rhythms for the first time, we had a shuffle beat on our first album, it's like the first time we'd ever used a shuffle beat. By the time you get to your third album, you know, you've covered some ground. Birds of a Feather is a vibe or a sort of approach to a song that is new for us, it is novel.

## Chunk 2

I think by that token though, out of context, even though we knew we loved it, and obviously it's done very well as a single, which we're super grateful for, like we didn't feel that it necessarily was the best representation before everybody heard the whole album. To us, when we made this album, we were really caring about it as a cohesive, you know, 44 minute piece of music that had highs and lows and a real dynamic range. And so the idea of like putting out Birds of a Feather five weeks before anybody heard the rest of the album, we worried that it might misrepresent the album. And so that was one of the reasons that we chose not to put any singles out.

## Chunk 3

And you know, people's taste is powerful. We didn't push Birds of a Feather on anybody, and it still had this unbelievable life. It's like Billy's biggest song now, and I think that's just a real testament to the fact that the audience is really in control. They heard the album and they singled out Birds of a Feather and they've given it this life. And now there's a music video for it, and you know, Billy closes her shows with it. But I think it has a lot to do with the relationship that the audience organically had with the song, as opposed to some kind of manufactured effort to make a song very popular.

## Chunk 4

I can't speak for Billy, but I didn't feel a pressure in terms of sales or something. I just really wanted to make something that I was really proud of. And I think sometimes the harder I work on things, the more proud of them I am. So I just wanted to work really hard and make sure that we were making the most sort of carefully thought through body of work.

## Chunk 5

As a little last addendum to this, I'll just pan over it. There are so many things on the cutting room floor I'm going to pan down to just countless muted, hidden files. These are, you know, things that didn't make it. Drum tracks, bass tracks, synth tracks that just didn't make the cut. Versions that fell by the wayside didn't work out. And that's okay. You know, that wasn't a waste of time. We learned a lot about what we liked and also that everything got kind of cherry picked. There's lots of things from those versions that I pulled over into the kind of final version. But yeah, we definitely felt a lot of pressure. We were really, we really put our all into this album. It's definitely our favorite album we've ever made.

## Chunk 6

I think the most luxurious way to work on something is to give yourself a structure or a schedule that isn't too intense and to just follow that structure. So on this album, we worked three days a week, Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, or Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, depending. And we do that every week. There were days that were very productive and days that weren't productive at all and days that were long and days that were short, but we just always did those days. And so, you know, three days a week times, you know, 52 weeks. You're talking a hundred and, you know, 50 odd days. So you get a lot done in 150 days working on something. And you get to be really careful and thoughtful and you don't have to feel like you're running out of time.

## Chunk 7

There were no kind of compromises on this album because we ran out of time. Sometimes you do own an album. You make something for two weeks and it has to be done. There's, you know, ah, we never got time to record those strings or something like that. And you just have a compromise. And those are fine that sometimes compromises can be really cool and inventive. But in this case, it feels really good to have made an album that was very uncompromising.

## Chunk 8

John Costelli and Aaron Forbes co-mixed this album and they're both super talented guys. And Billy and I are both in the room for a lot of the mixing process. I kind of am a mix-as-I-go person, you know. I think, like, this session file doesn't sound tremendously different than the final mix. Ultimately, I think that John and Aaron's mixes are better and more balanced, but this is pretty close and pretty baked, so it's definitely something I'm very mindful of. I don't know if my production is good if I haven't done a good mix, you know what I mean? Like, I don't know if it's done yet. I think that's, you know, certainly an element, but I thought John and Aaron did a really great job mixing this album.

## Chunk 9

I think I have my own set of ears in my own studio, in my own car, and those are biased, you know what I mean? I might make the mix that sounds the best to me in my car or my living room, but I want it to sound good for everybody everywhere. One of the things that John does that I think is a really great mixing practice, he, like, you send him a reference mix and he balances it so that his stems sound exactly like your reference mix and then he goes from there, do you know what I mean? Sometimes when you work with a mixer and you go like, I like the reference better, they have to change a bunch of stuff to even get it close to your reference. It's, he gets it to a place where he's already sounds just like the reference you have and then he, you know, enhances a bunch of stuff. And so anytime you go like, I missed this from the reference, he goes, but presses a button and it's right back to how the reference sounds, which I think saves everybody a lot of time.

## Chunk 10

Once again, I'm Phineas. It's been a treat to be with Mix With The Masters today here at East West Studios in Hollywood. Hope you enjoyed going through the tracks for the song Birds Of A Feather. Hope you enjoyed seeing this full video. I'll be sure to see you next time.
