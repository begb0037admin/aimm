## Chunk 9

You could tell that the rough is really blown out and distorted, but there's a little, there's more fidelity and there's, even then, there's more impact and punch. It's not just one thing, it's an accumulation of multiple small incremental moves that get you from here to here.

## Chunk 10

The one first thing is, it's always gain staging. I'd say at this early step of the record, it's gain staging it right to, if I'm not getting rid of what they have, I'm gain staging it right. If I'm getting rid of what they have, it's regain staging it, but it's mainly gain staging because a lot of what you hear, yes, the 808's blown out, the instruments have that clip sound to it, but a lot of the pumping and a lot of that is just stuff being fed in so that everything is working double time.

## Chunk 11

So you have your first compressor on the vocal, whatever, just let's just say Arvox for the Renaissance compressor, that would be, that's doing too much. Then the next one you have, you have an 1170's, you have, all these things are all doing so much work that it just all just sounds like everything is compressed, there's no transients, there's no dynamics, everything is kind of like that. And what we have to do is get it there so that there's, you know, movement and breathing going on.

## Chunk 12

When you get a rough mix, you're going to listen to it and see, and like sometimes it's right there and all you have to do is a little bit of enhancement and sometimes you have to see that, okay, cool, this is, this is what it sounds like. How do I still, still convey the same message but do it a little more tastefully? And sometimes that's just what it is, that's just meant to be super blown or distorted and that's what you go with.

## Chunk 13

But for the most part, like when I get mixes or roughs and stuff, the one thing that I've noticed a lot of times is the vocals are over compressed, like it's audible compression. And I'm not talking, you know, like upwards of 15, 20 dB, I've seen even more at times and it comes from everything is pegging, you know, the gain reduction meter that's just built into Pro Tools, just goes all the way down. So I get why people do it, like I said, it helps you push it, push it forward a bit. But the first thing I'll do is I'll get rid of all that compression and then I'll clean up the vocal, the raw vocal. I'll do clip effects, clip gain, subtractive EQing, just whatever I can do to clean it up before it gets compressed. So it hits the compressor at a more uniform tone, but that's what it is, just over compressed vocals and, you know, whatever you do to deal with them.

## Chunk 14

Another one I see a lot is, which I spoke about just before this, is wrong gain staging, but wrong gain staging leading into a mix bus. And these days there's so many of these plugins that really easily get you loud really quick. There's a lot of sacrifices that are made in getting you to that destination. So a lot of times they have these plugins on the rough mixes that have gotten them really loud, but it's completely blown out and distorted. So now it's how do we take that, clean it up, add transients, carve space out, just make room for everything to fit and not be so smushed together.
