## Chunk 1

Okay, so we're gonna jump into J. Cole's vocal. Again, when you're getting vocals from a camp, like a J. Cole camp, a Drake camp, from Chris Brown, myself, when we send out vocals, we don't send them out raw. And most big artists will never send out raw vocals. So what I'll do, for example, in my case with Chris, is if we have a record that he's doing a feature on, I will mix his vocal and then that will be sent as a two-track to another mixer if someone else is mixing it besides myself. In this case, it's the same thing. I was mixing the record for T. Grizzly. He got J. Cole as a feature. The vocals came as a two-track mix from J. Cole. What I needed to do then is get that vocal to be able to fit inside of my mix properly, but then also maintain as much of the vibe and the feeling and the sound from what their team created. So you guys are gonna see my processing on his vocal is a lot more limited than when I'm doing a raw vocal like with T. Grizzly, which we're gonna see here in a second when I get to his vocal.

## Chunk 3

## Chunk 4

One question that I get a lot about how I create these notches, especially something like down here. Let's play really quick and see what this graph is telling us. When I pull up the EQ, I'll also use the graph as a tool just to get a guidance, right? So with my ears, I'm sort of finding like it's kind of in this area. And when I pull up the EQ nine times out of 10, I'm immediately going to see the issue. It'll be that one bump that's just out of control in the low end. And so that's how I'm creating these notches and I'm adjusting the cues based on what I need. You can see here I'm doing tight cues and then on the low end, I'm doing broader, broader cues just to sort of shave because a lot of this is just shaping. I'm just kind of shaping everything almost like someone is shaping clay to make a statue.

## Chunk 5

That's the EQ for Jay and I'm doing a couple of high end notches here. I think I was trying to get some of that like mouth sound and Essie sound out a little bit, which there's also a de-esser, which I'll jump into in a second. But all of this helps sort of do it. I don't want to rely on one de-esser to attack all of my top end stuff. I want to use a little bit of EQ and then possibly some multi-banding and then I'll hit it with the de-esser. I don't want to make one plug-in work too hard. A lot of times you'll see I use multiple compressors on a vocal like an 1176. And then there's also a Pro C2 and a Soothe, which could be considered a compressor as well in some way. So I feel like you don't want to give all the work to one plug-in.

## Chunk 8

And after that, just the C2. So I've bypassed doing stuff like EQP1A on this 1176. This is already probably ran through compression and everything. Mixing J. Cole's vocal on this section was more about maintaining exactly what they had than me trying to say, okay, I need to fix all these things about it. I'm sort of taking the trust from the artist and allowing it to guide me. I feel like a lot of times in the past when I've tried to overstep and EQ too much out of something that came printed with processing from the artist, just the artist always came back and they were like, hey, you know, can you make it sound like what we sent you essentially. So I've learned my lesson in that. And I'm sure you guys have dealt with stuff like that too, where you kind of try to do what you think is right. And in the end, the artist just wants to sound the way that they had it before, but better.

## Chunk 11

Let's say the client comes back and they say, hey, this sound needs to be turned up three dBs. And I try the three dBs and I go, well, that's way too much. I'll do two and I'll send it to them. And I'm not going to be like, hey, here's three dBs up. I'll just say, hey, what do you think of this? And nine times out of 10, it's either good to go or they just say, oh, just do a little bit more. And I'll do like 0.3 or 0.4 more. I won't try to get to that full three.

## Chunk 12

There's been times where I've had to get a little bit over the line on what I'm comfortable with, but it's because what they wanted was so drastic that the meeting point in the middle actually was still passing where I felt it was, you know, necessary. I just mixed Nikki Nicole's album with a great producer Tatool. There was a lot of times where he was like, this needs to come up six dBs. And I'm just like six dBs. And it was like a balance, right? Where I'd be like, all right, two and a half, three dBs, see what that feels like. And if he gives me like one more up, I'll go another half dB. But that came from me having a great relationship with the producer.
