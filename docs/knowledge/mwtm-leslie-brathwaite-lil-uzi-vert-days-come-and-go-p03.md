# Days Come And Go — Part 3: Low End Kick Bass Foundation

## Chunk 1

I may listen. So right off top, I know there's a bass and an 808 and a kick. There's some things happening in there. I want to really, I want to get that right. I want that to be good stuff in there. So I'll probably focus on. So what we have is we're going to have a kick. That's the main body of the driving force of the low end. Then we're going to have a bass line, but it contains a lot of low end information. And then there's a bass pad. And the function of the pad is going to be to really get the notes through. The bass line gets the thickness of the bass through, but the bass pad is going to make sure we can hear the notes.

## Chunk 2

So first thing we got to do is we have to get the kick feeling right. So we're going to start with that. First thing I wanted to do is make sure this kick had some punch to it. So I went to a transient designer to really bring out the frequency, the chest frequency that I wanted. So right away you hear that chest frequency punch too. And then it's about making sure it's really heard and felt with the Pultec EQ. All of a sudden, different energy. And then I came again with the transient EQ because I even wanted more of that chest boxy frequency out because I know I have to push through a lot of low end on the basses. So just really making sure I'm tapping through on that top end part of the kick to really punch through all the bass that I'm about to deliver.

## Chunk 3

I will mute up the kick. I will go to the most prominent bass and see what it's doing. And a lot of times I'm going to probably have to. Because it needs to have a presence and a really big low presence in your face, I'll start with the Fairchild 670 Legacy. And I'll just throw that bad boy on there. Right away, presence. And then I'll go for the big guns. Little Labs, Voice of God. To give it that body, that meat. And then Pultec. At this point, with a bass, a lot of times I'll use 30 hertz on the Pultec so that I can really boost that roundness and that thickness because I know the kick is going to be coming in at some frequencies above that 30 hertz. So I'm really going to boost that on the bass. And then see how they work together.

## Chunk 4

And then I got to see what the other sound, the bass pad, was doing. So that sound is giving us more space than anything. If you notice, when I bring in the bass pad, it takes the bass from being right here to spreading it out. All of a sudden, the bass feels like it's around us now. And it's just giving that all-encompassing type feeling.

## Chunk 5

Now I want to check the hook. And we still want that kick to have some energy, but we now want to respect the fact that it went to a higher register. So still trying to incorporate some of the low-end energy, but understanding that bass in particular is meant to kind of reach up as far as frequencies. And that's going to be because there's a lot of stuff happening in that hook that's going to take up a lot of that space. And you'll see what I mean.

## Chunk 6

And now we go to the snares and the claps and make sure that those things, once you've got the bass, the kick, and the snare really driving a song, everything else is candy. To get my snares smacking a bit, again, sometimes you can use the same tools for different things because that Fairchild 670 Legacy, it's compression, it's limiting, and it just really brings things up in your face. Immediately, that snare jumps up in your face. And then I wanted some body on the snare, so I boosted a lot of low-end. Same thing with the clap. You're going to hear the clap jump up. That clap supports the first tap of the snare. And then same thing with the hats. Just make sure they're in there, the rolling hats. I like to boost the high-end with the precision EQ to make sure those hats are really cutting through. And this is kind of like a hat compilation track. Rolled off some low-end with the Pro EQ and then with the Pro Q3 and then added a lot of high-end with the precision EQ again.

## Chunk 7

Now, at this point, when I get the drums, kick, and bass, you know, when I get them sitting where I like, that's when I start going to my mix bus and I will throw on this plug-in that I like. I learned about this plug-in from David Guetta. I was mixing the album, a Rita Ora album, and I noticed all the mixes were coming over with this plug-in on them, and I would deactivate it and try to see, like, what he did and why he was using it. So, at this point, when I got the meat of the song together, I will then throw this plug-in on here and then mix through this plug-in from now on. So now I want to make sure that this plug-in is going to generate the hit and the meat of where the song is going to get to as far as the glue, everything that brings the song together in loudness, in just a function of making it really sit up. So, you'll hear it right away when I activate it, and then I'll mix through the plug-in, you know, continually. So, this is it going in. Now, all of my decisions are made with that plug-in activated and going forward, and that just kind of gives me a nice sense of where I need to be.
