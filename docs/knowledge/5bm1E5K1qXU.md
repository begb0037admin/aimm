Getting to know your environment is key for a great mixing experience. Frankly, it doesn't matter if you have a high-end setup or you're mixing with your laptop and headphones; if you don't know your monitoring, you will be left confused about the overall quality of your mix and the way it will translate across different speaker systems. For example, you could have a great pair of high-end monitors but sit in a room where there's a lot of echo, or maybe you're sitting in a standing wave where the low end is boosted or attenuated. Not being aware of these issues could result in mixes that are quite unbalanced. Knowing how professional songs sound within your setup will help tune and calibrate your ears to your mixing environment and your monitoring setup. I played back mixes that I knew really well to help me calibrate the EQ settings on the back of my monitors, and all those little details in getting to know your setup really matter to accurately predict how these mixes might translate on different devices. Timing, tuning, and removing unwanted noise will go a long way for a clean, polished, and punchy mix, so be sure to fix those problems before starting your session. For example, if your kick and bass are coming in on the first beat but are slightly out of time with each other, that will affect the impact of that first hit, and it's not going to be your dynamics processing or EQ that will solve it. Sometimes just nudging them a little bit more in time with each other will do the trick. Unwanted noise during your takes can distract from the song, and they can also be amplified when you apply compression, so reach for RX11 to solve those issues before you dive into the mix. I like to use Voice De-noise to clean up my vocal recordings or Mouth De-click to get rid of unwanted clicks. I also like to use Guitar De-noise for unwanted amp noise or string noise that I want to minimize. Don't ignore the power that tuning can have on your mix too. Sometimes a great vocal could use just a little bit of tuning adjustment to prevent sharper notes from poking out of your mix. Taking the time to prepare your session before you dive into the mix will create a great workflow that will allow you to work faster and more efficiently. Ultimately, you want to get these right for a mix that sounds well-balanced.
