## Chunk 3

So the first thing I did was I pulled up the you know obviously different computers moving around. We all know how much auto tune switches around. So I set the auto tune as soon as I got it and I right off the bat after putting the key in I was like something doesn't seem right. And I realized after going to a different computer and running the original session that the tracking engineer originally put the key in wrong. Instead of minor it was a major. Obviously that's that's not correct. So I fixed it right off the bat. And then when I played the first version for Thug and everyone in the room they were like yo what happened. They sang into it the wrong way and it does this cool thing with third note it corrects it and it's doing some weird harmonic stuff that because everyone got used to it. It did feel weird it being missing. So that's one of those things where it was actually done as a mistake but the artist lent into it and it created this effect. And that just goes to show that a lot of times things are done and you trying to fix things too much you will lose a gig or you will mess it up. That wasn't done intentionally but they they used it and then once I took it away it took away from the feeling of the record that everyone was used to listening to so much. So I had to keep it and just just use it as it is. And you see a lot of the stuff that I'm doing over here is just filtering and leaning into the mix bus. Like I said I'd planned on going to LA and mixing this and like you know redoing it in my studio but it just it worked and everyone liked it. So we we kept it and that's that just goes so that it's more about the song and the record as a whole rather than all these technical things that we do. And really you just got to make the artist happy.
