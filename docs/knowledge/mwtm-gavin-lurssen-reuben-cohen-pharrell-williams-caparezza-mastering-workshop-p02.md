## Chunk 1

The console that we designed and built is a passive console, and it's designed to be invisible in the process but allow us control of the gain structure. That really is the gist of it. The modifications to each piece of gear essentially add up to the clipping of op-amp. So gear that's designed to be used in balanced environments and have particular amplification processes throughout their circuitry have been cleaned up, and that's been taken away so that we can work in an unbalanced environment and clean up the audio. That's really the gist of these modifications. In some cases, there's been some resistors added so that they can properly feed our console at correct gain structures. The way we approach the art of mastering is maybe a little bit different than how most people understand the process. It's not a situation where we're adding one process and then committing and then maybe adding another process. That way of working might work for some, but it's never the way we've worked. What we like to do is we like to first listen to the mix and envision that ending up in a balanced, finished place, ready for the market. How we go about doing that is we have to really, really know our room, we have to know our speakers, we have to know our signal path, we have to have a very strong connection to the processing in itself. We have to have the ability to know how the signal can go through the process in a very, very clean, transparent way as a starting point. And then from there, decide to build on that and add some saturation, add some EQ adjustments, add some compression as needed. And that just takes experience and really, really knowing your process. So as we're working, the process itself informs our decisions as much as we're informing how to manipulate the gear as one global process. Every process in the signal path works in conjunction with every other process. So it all fits together like a puzzle piece that feels very natural and musical. And anything that's in the way of that needs to go. So that's as part of the process as anything else. Deciding how much gain from one piece to another piece from those pieces together to feed the rest is all part of the musical balance. And hitting that just right can be the thing that just feels completely like you can end up in a place where you're connected with the music. If you don't hit that just the right way, it can create slivers of disconnect. Somebody might not say anything, but they might not feel as connected to the music. So that's something that we're very, very sensitive to. You've got to know the genres out there. You've got to understand how the elements within the mix are working and what can be introduced to enhance that. And certainly knowing what not to do so you get in the way.

So I always feel that it's very important to keep as many things the same as possible. If you have your room set up, don't change the furniture. Don't change the couch in the back of the room. It's part of the room. Keep your speakers positioned in a particular way, in a way that's preferred for how they're interacting with the room, because all of these things are going to inform your decisions. In addition to that, references are very, very important. Having, you know, five to ten references in maybe various genres, you know how those translate in various environments. You know how they sound in the car, and in headphones, and Apple earbuds, and then on your monitors, too.
