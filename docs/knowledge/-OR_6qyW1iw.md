## Chunk 1

Hey guys, it's Sean Castelli here. Going to open up and break down the session for Birds of a Feather by Billy Eyish. Lucky enough today to have Aaron Forbes in town, my dear friend who co-mixed the album with me. This whole album, Billy went so deep on vocals, right? If you alter it, it starts to become you're like, well, that's not the person I know and love. I don't know what I'm crying for. I assume we have the G on this one for her. You should talk about that compressor. That's what's holding her forward with a little bit of top end boost and low-end body on the 200. We would just AB different boxes like SSLG channel versus J channel, Ozone tape versus maybe like a Kramer tape. Just these like emulation boxes that give tonal shifts and character to things without necessarily changing it. Billy's favorite part is when that bass comes in. John gassed it in the best way. I was like, man, this is the energy between the top end of the bass. How loud can it be without competing with her? I think that's why I try to talk about the value of the co-mix because historically you would have the producer in the room, the artist in the room, you'd have four hands on the console, six hands on the console. We don't have that anymore. Having him on the sofa and trading places with me at the desk, I instantly fell in love with this seat because the low end made sense to me. There's things you can hear up there I can't hear back here and vice versa.
