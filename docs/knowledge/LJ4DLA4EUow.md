## Chunk 1

So the mistake that myself and most other producers have made at some point is you work really hard on your production and you get to the mixing stage and you want every detail that you worked on so hard to be heard. So you start just turning everything up in the mix and it becomes this big wall of sound. So today's video is gonna be all about how to mix a busy track that has a lot of layers, and I'm gonna use this song as an example. So these are the raw stems that a client sent me to mix. So obviously it's not mixed yet. I'm gonna show you what it sounds like after mixing, but I wanna show you how many layers are going on here. So just looking at the drums, there are a ton of percussion layers driving the rhythm, lots and lots of layers, some low toms competing with the kick. Then we've got lots of different bass sounds fighting for the same space and that's before we even get to all these synth layers. So how can you possibly balance all those layers together and not make the mix sound muddy? Well, that's what this video is all about. So this is the final mix. I'm gonna show you what it sounds like right now and then I'm gonna go through how you can balance a busy mix. By the way, this is a track from an artist named Christian Carter. I'll link his Spotify page below so you can check out the song when it's released. So the first thing we gotta do actually has to do with human psychology and that's the fact that humans, when we're listening to any section of any song, we only really hear or remember one element at a time. The rest of the elements we kind of just feel, so what we gotta do is establish a hierarchy and actually write it down so we can focus on making our mix follow this hierarchy. So in this track I would say the vocals are definitely the number one thing that people are gonna remember in the drop. So then after that, what comes next? So I would say there's this lead synth layer underneath the vocals that sometimes when the vocals have a break, we want that to come through and be the second most memorable layer. So I'll write lead synth as the second layer. Then what's really driving the track and giving it power? That would be the drums and bass. And then we have our supporting synths, which those are supposed to be more felt than heard, like they're helping the overall vibe of the track, but you don't want those really high in the mix 'cause they're gonna make everything muddy and give it that wall of sound effect where nothing is really coming through. Then within each of these groups, you're also kind of gonna have a hierarchy too.
