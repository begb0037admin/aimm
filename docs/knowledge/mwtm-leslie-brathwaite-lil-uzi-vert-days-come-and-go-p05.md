It's important to understand how all the melodic things are moving, there's melodies in the bass, there's melodies with the lead line, there's melodies in his vocal and they are all working together and moving together, it's important to make sure that that's maintained. That's the other thing I'm listening for. Insecurities make me wish I was taller, I back down on the opportunity, I'm a staller, insecurities every night. All that movement needs to be heard and felt because even in the bass line you're hearing and then in the lead line you're then hearing a different type of movement, you're hearing so those two working together, creating that movement, then his vocal is moving, his vocal is doing other melodic movements. So all that needs to make sense as you mix and it's really about just feeling it, make sure the feel is there.

## Chunk 4

So the main difference, you can hear it already is with the rough, his vocal is a lot more raw, there's way more reverb on his vocal and it's a little more muffled. With my mix, I back down on some of the effects a bit to let his vocal really shine through top end clarity and my approach was right because he liked the mix but for me it was about the intimacy of what he was saying, I wanted that to be a lot more clear instead of washed out in reverb. So even if we go to the hook, let's listen to the rough and the hook. And then that's the rough and then this is clarity.

## Chunk 5

And what I noticed with the rough too, and this happens a lot with roughs, the artist a lot of times will push the engineer and tell them to really make it loud and the rough is slightly distorted. And so that's the other thing I'm trying to do too is make it to where the mix isn't as or isn't distorted at all, but still keeping that high energy, still keeping it loud. So the tracking engineers, a lot of times they don't have the time to work with it the way I do. The artist is standing right over them, tell them make it loud, make it loud, make it loud. So a lot of roughs I get are very distorted as opposed to trying to tame that distortion.

## Chunk 6

And I'll play another section of the song really quick. I'll play this breakdown section just so you can kind of hear. So that's the rough. So it's really just again about that clarity, about backing down on some of the reverbs. A lot of things sound a little more chaotic in the rough. I wanted to kind of dial that back and make things sound a lot more intimate, you know, and that was what I was trying to achieve with the mix.

## Chunk 7

In certain situations with rough mixes, what you have is you have some clients who can understand the explanation of, hey, I print my mix at a certain level. It's not going to be as loud as the rough. Certain clients, you know they can lock into the technical aspects of that. Those clients I have that conversation with. But the ones that I know that's not going to make sense to them and all they're going to do is compare the mix to the rough. And if the mix just doesn't sound as loud as the rough, they're going to hate it. Then I have to take on that challenge of trying to make the mix match the loudness and the fierceness of the rough and just trying not to distort.

## Chunk 8

And there are times when in order to get to that level that the rough is at, I do have to distort a little bit. And again, that's relative to the artist. It's relative to the genre. If I'm mixing something that's straight hip hop or something gritty like a Lil Uzi Vert, a little distortion is okay. Distortion isn't the worst thing ever in a Lil Uzi Vert record. Distortion is a terrible thing in a Beyonce record. So I have to kind of every situation, every artist, the song, the type of song, the genre, all of those factors matter in how I approach the loudness wars, how I approach am I going to make it sound just as loud as the rough or am I going to have this explanation and tell them, hey, X, Y, and Z.

## Chunk 9

Sometimes, again, I only have that conversation with people who I know can receive that conversation. The people who I know can't receive that conversation, I don't even bother. I just take on the challenge of trying to make it loud. So the loudness wars is a thing. But for most people with the loudness wars, what I usually tell them is a lot of times a client will call me and they'll say, hey, it's not loud enough. The mix sounds good, but it's just not as loud as the thing I'm listening to in the club or that. I'll be like, man, you have a volume knob. Just turn it up. And then I explain to them that that's what mastering does. So a lot of times that conversation tends to work.

## Chunk 10

And, yeah, again, for me, it's a quick process. It's going through. A lot of people always ask me, how do I know when I'm finished? For me, as I listen to this, when I know I'm finished with a mix, with creativity, you can always ask yourself a few questions in a row. When I start asking too many questions in a row, when I start saying to myself, hey, could that have been up or should I have turned that down or maybe I should have messed with it? When I find myself messing with stuff too much or asking questions about what decisions I made, that's when I know I'm finished.

## Chunk 11

Because I say this all the time. You can unmix a song just as much as you mixed it. You can sit here and mix the song and then you can unravel the song by making all kind of different decisions. So I try to just limit how deep I dive into moving this and changing this. And I know I'm pretty much good. And I feel it. I play it through. I feel it. And I know I'm good with the mix. And that's pretty much where I end up on a mix like this. I'm pretty much at the point where I love it. I send it to him. And then it's about being open to whatever changes he may have.

## Chunk 12

Because that's the other part of the mix process. It's a collaboration. Other people may have input. The producer, the artist, person from the label. In my opinion, what keeps me as a successful mixer as well is I'm able to be open to all of that input. And making sure that we're making a collaborative thing that we all love and we all feel good about.
