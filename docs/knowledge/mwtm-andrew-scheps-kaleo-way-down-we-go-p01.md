# Way Down We Go — Part 1: Studio Session Pre Mix Preparation

Source: MWTM set record: docs/mwtm/sets/Andrew_Scheps_Kaleo_Way_Down_We_Go_Mixing.md (local recording, no public URL)
Channel: Mix With The Masters

## Chunk 1

Hello, I'm Andrew Sheps and welcome back to Rue Boyer, the fabulous mix from the Masters studio in Paris. Today we're going to have a look at a song called Way Down We Go by Kaleo. One of the things that's interesting about this song is actually how I had to interact with the band before we even started mixing. So Jakir King had produced the original tracks for this album and I don't know the exact story but I think the album was taking quite a long time not because of Jakir but the band is very very meticulous and you'll see as we look through the session there are lots and lots of printed effects and things like that.

## Chunk 2

The project was coming to me and this was going to be the first single, the rest of the record wasn't even finished yet. And I had a conversation on the phone with them and one of the first thing I asked them was well can I hear the rough mix before we start so I know what we're dealing with. And they said well there isn't a rough mix and to me that's a big red flag like hold on there's no rough mix everybody makes rough mixes.

## Chunk 3

And what it was is that they had taken all of the guitars and printed them through Leslie's and printed them through separate amps and as part of the mix process they wanted to go through all the options and figure out what worked best. And I just felt like in a way that wouldn't actually serve the band because they wanted to really be meticulous in deciding what they wanted to use and where whereas I would be trying to mix the song and to have those two things happen at once would be counterproductive.

## Chunk 4

So I said I think what you need to do is take your tracks, take them away somewhere and work until you can make a rough mix. And so that's exactly what they did. They booked a studio and they went in and I think it was three days of going through the possibilities and making the arrangement. And I'd love to say that that's really shaped the way they make records from then on. I don't know about that at all.

## Chunk 5

But one of the things you will see is how many printed effects there are. We'll talk about some of the minute details of the mix. Like at the end, those repeated go, go, goes, those are not delays, those are all sung. And getting the contour of the volume as those would disappear and the reverb would come up a little bit, very, very meticulous. We spent probably as much time on that as anything else in the mix.

## Chunk 6

And one of the other things that happened along the way is, and I don't know where in the process this happened, but the drums were bounced. And they're not just a mild, rough mix of the drums. They're pretty processed and in a really cool way. And it's an interesting place to start a mix, to have something that usually is the thing you spend the most time shaping and getting together. And you've got lots of microphones and you try to make it sound like one instrument and one musician to have that kind of taken care of, but also in a way where you can't change it and it's not subtle.

## Chunk 7

So we can start off, I'll just play a little bit of the drum stem and it's fantastic. I think it sounds great. And it absolutely informed everywhere else I was going to go with this mix. So here's a little bit of the drums. So it sounds great. There's some reverb on the snare. The floor tom is a huge part of this. And as we get later in the song... I mean, it's distorted and there's a vocal effect, but that's fine.

## Chunk 8

And it was partly a challenge, but also it really freed me up because I didn't have to make decisions about the drums. Now I still did some processing. There's a lot of clip gain section to section. You'll notice that there's a copy of the drums bounce, which I call drums parallel because it was basically what I would do with parallel compression. I just did it with plugins on one track because it was the easiest way to do it. And then also I'm doing some kick reinforcement.

## Chunk 9

And for anyone who's seen videos of me in the last so many years, I will trigger Sampler Live using something like AP Trigger, which is one of my favorites. So this is pre that. I'm using Battery and it was actually Battery 3 or something like that. And I used Massey DRT to create this MIDI. So that is a plugin by Massey who make fantastic plugins and it was an audio suite plugin where you give it audio and it generates a MIDI file.

## Chunk 10

And then what was cool about that is when you were editing the MIDI, I'm just going to move this up underneath the kick and let's blow it up a little bit. With the MIDI, you can just trim the beginning or the end of the note. So I can move the beginning of the note right on where the kick drum is and tighten up the timing of the MIDI in a much easier way than when I used to use Sound Replacer beforehand when you would end up with one long audio file and you would have to chop it and fix all the timings that were there.

## Chunk 11

The other thing that's quite cool is that it generates velocity based on the volume of the audio that's there. So you can see that kick is quieter than that kick and the velocity, well that's a snare, but the velocity is down. So it follows the dynamics of the kick drum quite well. And then what I would often do, and oh I did it on this, is I could take the triggering of the sample and move it around just by delaying the MIDI.

## Chunk 12

But more importantly, I could use the real-time properties of the MIDI track to basically compress the kick drum. So I'm only using 66% of the velocity that was put in there. So it would have been 100 higher, so 27 to 127. Now it only goes up 66, so I'm technically compressing that. And then I can offset it by putting it up 34. So without that, you'll see everything dives down. There are actually bigger gaps between the velocities on the track. And this lets me compress the triggering of the sample because it's very problematic to do that afterwards.

## Chunk 13

So it's a great way to trigger. The only reason I don't do it now is it takes longer than putting AP Trigger in. And AP Trigger does the same sort of thing. It's easy for me to adjust the timing. It's easy to adjust how much it's reacting to the velocity and things like that. But, old school.
