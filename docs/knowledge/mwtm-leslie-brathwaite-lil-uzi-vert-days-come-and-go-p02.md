## Chunk 1

I usually start with some light de-essing, I usually start with what I don't like about a thing. I move from a very subtractive place where I'm taking away frequencies more than I'm adding, or before I'm adding, and then I go from there. So I'm just going to walk through it and I'll step through each plug-in as I play it and you'll kind of hear. So first, de-essing, I just like to start with just the basics, and then I move to light compression. Right away you hear him get tamed just a bit, and then it's about really chopping out some of the low end in his voice and bumping up some highs because I want the clarity and that real steel clarity to come through.

So now it's back up in your face, a little more body feels present, and still some more high-end. And now I want to de-ess to control some of that high-end.

## Chunk 6

And so they're pretty much all doing the same thing. These reverbs and delays and everything down here, these are what Ben sent and Ben had on the track, and then all I did, I sent them through an effects mains track, and I rolled off a little low end on them so that the effects, when they start to bunch up, when you start to get a reverb, a haul, a delay, another delay, a large verb, all these things coming as effects, I like to roll off some of the low end sometimes because some of that low end information gets lost and confused and it starts to get a little muddled. So I like to roll off some of that low end information to kind of keep it top-end-y, really hearing the effects, really feeling that reverb.

## Chunk 7

And again, once I finish with the vocals, I move to the instruments. One thing about me, you'll see if you've watched some of my other videos, or if this is your first time watching one of my videos, you'll learn that, for me, I like to move quick. I like to move through these mixes quick. I don't like to spend too much time making decisions. I think it's the best way to get the most authentic work. It's the best way to work off of instinct. When you work kind of quick, usually your first decisions, usually the first thing your mind tells you to do is the best thing.

## Chunk 8

Can't tell you how many times I've worked with producers or artists, they'll come in and I'll play it, and then we'll go through about, you know, 17 to 20-something different iterations of the mix. And at the end, they'll go, you know what, just put it back how you had it. Because the instinct is what really feels good at the end of the day. So I move through these mixes pretty quick.
