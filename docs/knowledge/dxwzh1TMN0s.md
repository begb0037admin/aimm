# Here's Why You Struggle To Finish Songs

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dxwzh1TMN0s
Channel: Make Your Music

## Chunk 1

For the first 6 years of making music, I never released a single song. And not because I thought they were bad songs, but because I was actually afraid of being judged. But once I fixed that part, everything changed. I had lots of ideas, hooks, chord progressions, and like full pages of lyrics and notes and ideas, even full songs, but nothing ever got past the finish line. And back then, I actually thought that the problem was time. I was working full-time, so music was just something I squeezed into the evenings and weekends, and I kept telling myself, "When I have more time, I'll release my music, or I'll finally make that album." But something else started to creep in a little bit, too. The thoughts of me saying like, "Well, maybe I'm just not good enough yet to share my music." I knew at the time I could sing. I had a good voice. I knew I knew some of the songs were good, but just finishing them and getting something out and putting it out into the world, that's where everything stopped. So, looking back now, the real problem was I cared just way too much about what people would think of me. I didn't even share songs with my wife at the time. I kept everything to myself. They were locked away in my notebook, or just like buried in an early GarageBand project. So, when I finally finally started sharing, I wouldn't even actually do it in person. I would send a text, so I didn't have to have that like initial feedback of face-to-face. That's how much I was protecting myself. And I think a lot of musicians get stuck in this exact trap. They think, "Okay, I'll release music when I'm better, or maybe when I have more time." But the truth is, you don't get better until you actually let people hear it. Everything changed, like everything changed when I started just like publishing music and getting real feedback. But here's the difference. I stopped getting feedback from just friends or family. I wanted to avoid that feedback that was like, "Oh, this is great or you're amazing." When you're really not. I wanted that constructive feedback. The feedback that kind of like stings a little bit, the one that say like this part actually sucks or this part needs improvement or this part doesn't work. It's the feedback that actually gives you direction and that's really when when things clicked for me. I also remember hearing like Ed Sheeran say something before um like describing turning on an old water tap. At first the water is muddy and it flows out all muddy, but if you keep running the water it eventually clears up and that idea really really stuck with me. It was then when I was like, "Okay, I'm going to stop treating every song like the one, like it has to be the hit and start seeing each song as just like one
