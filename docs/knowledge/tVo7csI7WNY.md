# 3 Rules to Finally Release Your Music Instead of Sitting on It

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tVo7csI7WNY

If you're on version 27 of your mix, then this video is for you. I've been mixing for almost 20 years, and honestly, one of the hardest lessons I had to learn wasn't EQ or compression, but it was how to finish a mix. When you do this professionally day in and day out, you don't get to just phone it in. Every mix is someone's dream, someone's baby, and I don't take that lightly. So, over the years, I've developed three rules that tell me with confidence when a mix is done. And in today's video, I'm going to tell you exactly what those three rules are. Let's jump in. Okay, so the first rule is the red flag approach. This is something I heard Manny Marquin talk about years ago and it is something that is guiding me through every single mix. The concept of this is is when I first play a mix, tons of little red flags go up, right? The vocals are too loud, the vocals are too muddy, the kick isn't kicking enough, um the snare sounds flat, this needs to be tuned, that needs to be vocaled, so on and so forth. So many things to fix on a mix and slowly you start attacking those, right? Fix up the vocals, fix up the kick and slowly but surely what I consider the red flags start going down as I play through the mix. And eventually you'll get to a point where when you play the song from the beginning, 30 seconds will go by, no red flags will go up and then suddenly you'll hear something and be like, "Oh, let me fix that." Go back to the beginning. And when you get to the point where you can listen from the beginning of the song till the end of the song and you don't have any little red flags go up, that's when I consider the mix done. Now, one little bonus tip to this red flag approach is many times we can get locked in on one section of the song. For instance, uh a lot of times when I first start mixing, I'll just play the chorus because that's where most of the instruments are playing and I can get a good balance of the whole mix with the chorus. But there are dangers in that. If we just keep listening to the chorus, we can lose concept of what the song actually is and start turning into mad scientist as opposed to someone who's trying to make a song that's going to be really good for the the listener to listen to. So once you get a good balance on your mix, I always think it's a good idea to start listening to the song in entirety. And that way you'll be listening to it as the listener will be listening to it as a full piece of work and you can really catch all the emotional cues of, you know, going from

The final rule is what I call the 24-hour rule or in some cases the 1 hour break rule. And the concept behind this rule is that fresh ears tell the truth. So for me, I usually mix a song and a half to two songs a day, depending on how big the song is and how many parts are there are. So, what I do is I start the first song in the morning and I put a timer on for an hour and I mix, you know, just in the flow, doing my thing, fixing all the red flags.
