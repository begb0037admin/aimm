# Therefore I Am — Part 3: Creative Collaboration Scheduling

## Chunk 1

When the lockdown started, I think I had schedule fatigue, where I'd been so hyper-scheduled for a couple years that I didn't want anything to do with a schedule, and I think it was actually our mom's idea. Billy and I were sort of like, sometimes we were working and writing a song, sometimes we were not doing anything, and our mom was like, what if you guys just... Put in your calendar that you were going to, Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday, you were going to always try to work on something together. You could change that up sometimes if you need to or whatever, but what if you just, in your brain, you thought like, alright, I'm going to always, on Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday, we're going to get together and make some songs. And I was like, that's stupid! I was like, quarantine is like, no, we're not doing anything, why would we need to have a schedule? And she was like, just try it out for like a week. And it was so incredibly helpful. Like the first week, we did Billy's song, My Future, and then a couple weeks later, we did this song.

## Chunk 2

And I think, yeah, I think, you know, when you have no sort of schedule from someone else, in my specific case, it has proven to be super helpful to just give myself a little bit of a schedule and go like, well, I'm going to try to do it today. And obviously, creativity is hard, right? You don't always write a great song that day. But yeah, we did find it very important to, to just put in our brains, like, okay, today you're going to come over, we're going to try to work on something. It's been so helpful. So that would be my tip would be like, just give yourself a little bit of an artificial schedule, if you need it.

## Chunk 3

And the other thing, which is like the exact opposite of that is like, I don't, when I'm like, some days I'm writing, I write like a really boring, lame, stupid song. And I used to put a lot of pressure on myself to like, write a great song every day. So if I was writing a song that wasn't good, I'd be like, oh my god, let me start over. I'll write a really good song. And it drove me crazy. And it wasn't very helpful. And as time went on, I was like, you know what, if I'm not writing anything good, then I'm allowed to stop. Like, I'm allowed to just go, you know, do the dishes or take a walk with my dog or call a friend. And that's really helped my sort of like, sanity in terms of like, creativity, it's made me much more effective to be like, oh, I'm not writing anything good right now. All right, I'm gonna go do other stuff.

## Chunk 11

The best advice I ever heard was from a mastering engineer named Dan Hirsch. Years and years and years ago, I was like 15. And he had said to me, it just matters that you like it. He was like, there's, there's so many, you know, professionals like engineers, mastering engineers, mixers, that'll tell you why what you're doing is wrong over the course of your career.
