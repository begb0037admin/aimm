I've been collecting all this gear, like neutrons and maestros, all these vintage pedals, as well as some newer pedals. Everything I do, I like to try to get the most natural sound as possible. And, you know, if I tried to mix in the box, I think I could do it. ## Chunk 3

The G console sounded great, but it had a particular sound. The J, when they came out, I felt it was more transparent, so I could easily sort of change my sound for different albums. Whereas the G, it sort of got stuck to this sort of punchy, a little bit boxy sound for my taste, which was great until I was on the J, and I realized how much cleaner the mix bus was as compared to the G. So I felt I could change my sound a lot easier than if I was on the G console. A lot of engineers use compressors and EQs on the mix bus. Back when I was first starting, I tried to use that because I was assisting engineers who were using a compressor and an EQ in the final stage of the mix bus. So I did try to incorporate that in my style of mixing. But to me, I didn't have as much flexibility as I wanted to because, you know, for instance, if I wanted to push the bass, I'd have to worry about how much I could push it before the compressor on the mix bus would start reacting too much. And so for me, I would lose dynamics, and I couldn't easily just turn up the bass when I wanted to or, oh, wait, the kick drum sounds a little low now. Let me just turn that up. And I kept feeling like it was like a push and pull situation. If I wanted to do something, I'd constantly have to tweak the mix bus compressor. So I started mixing without it. And for my dynamics, I rely more on riding the faders and individual compression in EQ. And so I've been doing that for many, many years. And once in a while, I'll use the SSL compressor. But even then, I would just do minimal processing with it, just getting rid of some of the transients that I might not be able to see on the meter or hear. But I feel I'm a lot more freer to change stuff doing rides without having to affect my two bus compressor and EQ. I'll patch the compressor close to the end of the mix, just to get maybe a little bit of gain that I can't get from the board. But then again, I'll just leave it to the mastering guys to get my levels up. So I end up with a super dynamic mix without having any sort of processing in the end. And the song we're doing today is called Till It's Done from Black Messiah. And I'm just going to have some fun with this track, mix it from scratch, and we'll see what happens. Today, I'll be using NS10 without any use of any subwoofers or anything. And I think in general, my philosophy is never to use any subwoofers unless the room actually needs it as part of the sound of the room.
