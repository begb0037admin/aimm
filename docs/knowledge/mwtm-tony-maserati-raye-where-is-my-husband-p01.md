## Chunk 1

Hi, I'm Tony Maserati, and I'm here at Rue Bollier in Paris for Mixed with the Masters. And I am going to be talking a little bit about Where is My Husband by Ray, produced or co-produced by Mike Sabath. You've all heard this song by now, so you have an idea of what's happening. There's no particular lead vocal. There are hundreds of vocal tracks and lots and lots of instrument tracks as well. This song was, like many challenging songs, something that you are constantly, I was constantly moving to catch up to. When you're given something like this, you're given a rough mix, and we'll play the rough mix today as well. And you say, oh, okay, I get it. You know, I understand it. I kind of get some ideas going. But when you then dig in and see all the material and the intricacies of vocal parts, it's just incredible. There's so much stuff going on.

## Chunk 2

In some ways, you have to kind of compartmentalize, which is not generally what I do. I mean, typically, I like to play all the tracks, everything at once, and just hack through it, hunt through it, find the things that I'm hearing in my head, and start working. But in something like this, it's like, okay, you know, I've got my folder for this section, I've got my folder for that section, and I'm working through those kinds of things, and just hiding things in folders so that I can just keep my wits about me. The excitement that the song provides immediately, I mean, as soon as the song starts, it's exciting. So that's one of the things that you've got to figure out how that emotional rollercoaster is going to work, right? And how you're going to support it, and then, of course, communicating with Ray and Mike about changes.

## Chunk 3

Throughout the process, I'd be mixing and sending drafts to Ray, and she would be very specific about exact parts. Oh, this note is coming out too much. That note needs to be gone. Let's just mute that note. Your husband is coming. You know, her ears is amazing, and her ability to know sections, exactly what's going on. She and I would do FaceTime while I was working, and then I would livestream to her so she could hear it while she was doing who knows what at the time, and she could hear it from across the room and know that note is too much, that part is dominant, and it shouldn't be, or that part, it should be dominant. And she would just be correcting as we go.
