# Gorilla — Part 3: Stereo Mono Sample Dynamics

## Chunk 1

So another big thing with the track as well is the push and pull of stereo and mono and how that's kind of utilised. Obviously with the sample redo, there was a real kind of easy way to essentially make it a stereo sample and how that could have become more of a big part of the song and it could have become about this big instrumentation that's also with this huge vocal. And actually what you then do is you then, you're losing the kind of that immediacy, that kind of like sharpness that you will get from a mono sample. There's something about it where when you listen to this song on headphones, it doesn't completely like envelop you. It doesn't take over your listening space. It still allows you as a listener to be a part of it. It makes you focus on Sims more. It allows Sims to sit on the track in a completely different way. She is not being swallowed up by this instrumentation. And so it was always a very, very big part of these verses. The only thing that actually is actually going along in a stereo way is just the plate and the noise. That's the only thing that's stereo in these verses. So you're just completely down the middle. There's no width at all.

## Chunk 2

What it does though is then you have the intro section, which is just a huge stereo, massive orchestral recording. It's completely wide. You hear this massive room. It shows you there's this huge space, but then it's completely taken away from you and just filled with her. And within the first 20 seconds of the song, you're given everything, then it's taken away and then you're given everything again, but in a new wave of the vocal. And so as a listener, you're pushing and pulling in and out. On the mix side, it would have been really easy to just, right, well, we started this massive sample, so let's just stay up. Let's just make it knock really hard. Let's make it a really big mix. But then I'm forgetting about the whole song. I'm not taking into account Sims, what she's saying, what, you know, how the song actually feels. I'm just thinking about what I want as a mixer.

## Chunk 3

By the kind of halfway through the first verse, you probably would have been exhausted and wanted to turn off the song. Sims' flow is just absolutely on point. But there's a release that you know can come again because you heard it at the beginning of the song. And so when you then get to the end of this first verse. It's a full release. You've got new drums that are added. You've got this same big orchestral recording. You've got Sims' ad-libs going over the top of it. You know, like this recording, which was done in the same session with Chris Dave when we were doing the actual sample is completely nuts. And so it's just like driven. It's wide. It's, you know, it's overemphasized.

## Chunk 4

And this point in the song is a key point in the song. The way that Inflow has produced this, the way that we worked on the mix, is to give that moment of like, there's a space, and then something new comes in. And this is where then stereo is present throughout the rest of the song.

## Chunk 6

I think, again, with the kind of the speed of the way that we work on these mixes, when we're working together, myself, Inflow and Sims, it would be really easy to start doing like little tricks and think, okay, I can, you know, maybe there's a few delays that could start building here. Maybe I can start building like a reverb as well. But that stuff is just going to cheapen the drop. You know, you want these things to be a surprise. You want them to feel immediate, organic. And for me, it's the biggest part of this mix was not doing things. Keep it raw, which actually can be really, really hard to do.
