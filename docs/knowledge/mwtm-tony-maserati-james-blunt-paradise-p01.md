So I'll put a note in my comments, just so I don't forget it. But the basic idea is, I don't like to slow my flow down. So I start by playing the whole song over and over, and I try not to do too much soloing. If I'm curious about where a sound is coming from, I might solo. But generally, I'm trying not to do too much soloing in my first few hours, so that I'm sort of massaging the song with everything together. Sometimes I'll mute the lead vocal, just because I want to hear the track first, and get a sense of what that's doing, once I know what the vocal is doing. I think that this is a very important aspect of how I do my job. When I was a younger engineer, I would immediately start soloing. I don't know why. And I find a lot of young engineers do the same. They'll solo for hours, and forget that there's a whole song involved. They'll worry more about whether or not the kick drum sounds good by itself, than whether it sounds good in the song. I spend as much time as I can listening to the whole song, or at least all the vocals, or all the instrumentation. And sometimes I'll solo groups of things. So I'll solo drums and bass. In fact, it's rare that I solo the drums without the bass. I want to hear how that bass is working with the drums, and how much space it's taking up. So if I'm doing any EQing on the drums, I want to make sure that's in relation to what that bass is doing as well. Too much soloing can can lead you down a path that you then have a mix that is not cohesive. I just did a mix for Faith Evans. I needed to spend a lot of time getting the drums and bass correct. There were some samples involved and that sort of thing. And it was about four days of tweaking the drums and the bass and some of the instrumentation in real small detail. And it took me four days to get to a place where I could listen to the track. And then I realized, oh, well, my kick, there were several kicks, but one of them was just way too loud. And in fact, I think a response from the label came back, or the producer, sorry, came back that my drums were too hard, because I had been focusing on them for so long. So I rely, I'm very collaborative with my artists. It's extremely important for me to get their comments. Some artists don't like it. Some artists think that they should receive a final mix, or what I think is a final mix immediately. I don't really work like that. Other people do. I don't. I want to hear their comments and make sure that their vision is part of what I'm doing.
