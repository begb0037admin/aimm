# The Vocal Mixing Mistake Almost Everyone Makes

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JQGzreWqbv4
Channel: Streaky

## Chunk 1

If your vocal sounds fine on its own, but it doesn't sit properly when the mix gets busy, this is normally why some words will drift back and other words just jump out too hard. Most people will try and fix that with just one compressor, and that's where things usually start sounding wrong cuz vocals are naturally uneven. Some words are quiet and some hit harder than the rest, and that's what makes them feel human. But when you've got a dense mix, that range becomes harder to control cuz the softer bits get masked and the louder bits just push too far forwards. When you're using one compressor, you're asking it to do too much at once. You're wanting it to catch sudden peaks, smooth out the performance, and still sound natural. That's three jobs. So, you either set it gently and the loud words still pop out, or you push it harder, and the vocals start sounding squashed. Cleaner way to do this is to split the work. It's not more compression, just clearer rolls. Two compressors in a row, both doing different things. You use the first compressor just to catch the loud moments. Set it like this as a starting point. High threshold so it's only reacting to the peaks. Anywhere between 6:1 and 10:1 ratio. Use a fast attack and a fast release. You should only see gain reduction on the loudest words because most of the vocals should pass through untouched. This compressor isn't about tone or energy. It's just stopping the sudden jumps. So now the second compressor does the opposite job. This one is controlling the average level. Try these for a starting point. a lower threshold so it's always doing a little bit ratio 2 or 3:1 attack medium release so it kind of lets go between words and phrases you're looking for steady gentle movement nothing too dramatic this is what keeps the vocal present without forcing it forwards because the peaks are already handled this second compressor doesn't have to work as hard and that's the key vocal still has movement it still feels alive but it stays in place in the mix so now let's listen to it in practice Maybe we could get a little crazy. Sometimes all that I need is all that I need is you. The vocal sounds controlled and clear without sounding squeezed. And that's what you're aiming for. One important thing, the order matters. If you smooth first and then try and catch the peaks after, the compressors will fight each other. always peaks first and then consistency. This isn't a trick or a preset. It's just giving each compressor one clear job. If your vocals keep feeling uneven in a busy mix, try using this before reaching for more EQ or processing. It works far more often
