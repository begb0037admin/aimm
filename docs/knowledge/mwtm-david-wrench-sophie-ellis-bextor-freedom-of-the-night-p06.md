## Chunk 1

That's pretty much it really for what's in there, but we'll have a look at the Master Bus, because it's a little bit different to what I'd usually do on the Master Bus. Not 100% different, but reasonably different, but let's take everything off and have a lesson. So the first thing on the Master Bus is the Pro-Q 3 filter, so I've boosted the low end. A bit of top end boost, taking the very low end off to just tighten it up, taking a bit of muddiness out here and taking a bit of harshness, like about 3.5k, I'm often doing something around 3k, 3.5k, because you get quite a lot of build-up around there, it feels harsh when you play it loud on club speakers, it's a bit much, so I'll try and tame that a bit.

## Chunk 2

I don't often do this on the mix, but this felt like it needed a bit more grit to it. It was a bit clean, so we've got a bit of distortion happening, and then we pushed it quite hard into the Master anyway, which is causing a little bit of clipping, but then this is pushing that more. I've set it to keep the lows going through, so I don't really want to be distorting the very low end, but the sort of mids and highs can just have a bit of saturation. It's not crazy, but it's enough that you can... This gives it a bit of grit, and it allows a bit more volume to come through.

## Chunk 3

This is what's happening with the mix bus compressor. Slow attack, fast release, and a 2 to 1 ratio, and it's just compressing up to about 4 dB, it's just holding it in there. But because I've got the attack fairly slow, that's allowing the transients through, because if we were to put this with a faster attack, it would kill the... even when it's still doing the foot, it's killing the attack in the kick. So, you want to be... I always like to let the kick come through, let the attack happen on the kick and snare. And then the fast release, so it's just holding it in instead of getting rid of the transients.

## Chunk 4

I never like it, especially if stuff comes back from mastering and there's been too much attack on the transient. It just feels like the groove goes out of the song. You want that punch coming through. Again, we've got the low end is being held in there. A bit more action happening on the higher up we go. Let's just see, it just helps it all bond together and pump a bit, and just a-b-ing it. Yeah, it just gives it more excitement when you put it in, especially like we're listening quite quietly in here. It suddenly starts to jump out of the speakers more with this, because it's just reacting a bit more to the track, to the groove. It's about getting those transients through, but everything else pumping in between.

## Chunk 5

And then this is how it will go to mastering. It was mastered by Matt Colton, who I work with a lot, and he uses, in general, my unlimited masters, although very occasionally we'll use limited. There's some other mastering engineers I work with, they like to work off my limited files. And I'll normally just put a limiter on because you always want to send it out, so it's at a comparable level to the finished product. I use this. I don't really think about it too much. I just pull up the settings until I'm getting... I think, overall, when it goes through the whole track, it's going to be about minus nine lofts.

## Chunk 6

So it's not stupidly quiet when people listen to it, and it doesn't get rejected from being just too quiet with it. But then, yes, some mastering engineers like to work from that. But I will always send the limited version to the mastering engineer so they know what has been signed off, they know what's been okayed, so that when they're doing their limiting, they can compare.
